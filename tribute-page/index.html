Dr. Fusakichi Omori

founders of seismology

Here's a time line of Dr. Omori's life:

  • 1868 - Born in Echizen
  • 1890 - Graduated from the Imperial University Science College (now the Faculty of Science at the University of Tokyo) with a major in physics. After that, he specialized in meteorology and seismology in graduate school, and under the guidance of the British seismologist John Milne, who was invited to Japan, he conducted research on the aftershocks of the 1891 Nōbi earthquake.
  • 1898 - Developed the world's first seismograph capable of continuous recording, the Omori-style seismograph.
  • 1899 - Showed that you can determine the distance to the epicenter from the duration of the initial tremor and published Omori's formula for epicentral distance.
  • 1923 - Left for Australia to attend the Pan-Pacific Scientific Conference. While I was away, the Great Kanto Earthquake occurred. When he learned that this earthquake had happened in Japan, he set out to return home, but after coming back, he was immediately hospitalized and passed away a month later.

"Official Oomori also points out that if an earthquake happens right beneath you, you might not be able to respond in time even after hearing the emergency earthquake alert. We have to fully recognize this and prepare for earthquakes, or else we'd be letting Dr. Oomori down."

--Takuo Okamoto, president of the Fukui Prefecture Disaster Prevention Association and a professor at Fukui National College of Technology
